Jaw pain and clicking: preparing for physiotherapy in Winnipeg
Overview
Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction affects the jaw joint and surrounding muscles, causing pain, clicking, and limited jaw movement. An assessment can help determine whether physiotherapy is appropriate for your jaw symptoms and whether dental or medical assessment is also needed.
What We Treat
Jaw pain and stiffness
Clicking or popping sounds in the jaw
Difficulty opening or closing the mouth
Headaches related to jaw tension
Ear pain or ringing (tinnitus)
Facial muscle pain
Bite alignment issues
Teeth grinding (bruxism)
Our Treatment Approach
Manual therapy to release jaw and neck muscles
Joint mobilization techniques
Postural correction and ergonomic advice
Custom exercise programs for jaw mobility
Stress management and relaxation techniques
Education on jaw mechanics and habits
Care Goals
Reduced jaw pain and discomfort
Improved jaw mobility and function
Decreased frequency of headaches
Better sleep quality
Enhanced ability to eat and speak comfortably
When to Seek Help
Persistent jaw pain lasting more than a few days
Difficulty chewing or opening your mouth
Frequent headaches or ear pain
Clicking or locking of the jaw
Pain that interferes with daily activities
What to discuss at a jaw pain assessment
Tell your physiotherapist when your jaw symptoms began and whether eating, talking or opening your mouth is difficult. Mention dental treatment, injuries and any advice you have already received. An assessment helps determine whether physiotherapy is appropriate or whether you should also see a dentist or doctor. Jaw pain has more than one possible cause; a clicking sound alone does not establish a diagnosis.
Planning care around daily activities
Bring examples of the activities you want to improve, such as finishing a meal comfortably or speaking at work. Ask which options are appropriate for your assessment findings, what you can practise at home, and how progress will be reviewed. Physiotherapy may include education, movement exercises and hands-on care when appropriate. Treatment is agreed with you; no single technique or fixed course is suitable for everyone.
Booking TMJ physiotherapy in Winnipeg
Mention jaw pain when contacting reception so the team can confirm the appropriate appointment and provider. Bring any relevant dental or medical recommendations. You can contact the clinic without a referral, but your benefits plan may require one for reimbursement. Ask about the assessment fee and follow-up fees before attending; direct billing does not guarantee full payment by your insurer.
