# Is Physiotherapy Tax Deductible in Canada?

Physiotherapy is not a deduction, but it can count toward the medical expense tax credit. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) lets you claim eligible medical expenses on your return, and it lists physiotherapists as authorized medical practitioners in Manitoba.

This article is general information. It is not tax advice. Check the CRA pages linked below or ask a tax professional about your own return.

## What you can claim

You can claim what you paid a physiotherapist for treatment, as long as you were not paid back for it. The CRA says you can claim only the part of an expense that was not reimbursed and will not be.

That means:

- If your insurer paid part of a visit, you can claim the part you paid yourself.
- If your insurer paid the full visit, there is nothing to claim for it.
- Premiums you pay for a private health plan are listed by the CRA as an eligible medical expense.

## Where it goes on your return

The CRA uses two lines:

- **Line 33099** is for you, your spouse or common-law partner, and your children under 18.
- **Line 33199** is for other dependants, such as adult children, parents or grandparents who depended on you for support.

## The 12-month rule

You do not have to use the calendar year. The CRA lets you claim expenses paid in any 12-month period that ends in the tax year, provided you did not claim them the year before. This can help when your treatment ran across two calendar years.

## Why small amounts may not change your tax

The credit only applies to the part of your total medical expenses above a threshold. The threshold is 3% of your net income or a fixed dollar amount the CRA sets each year, whichever is less. If your total medical expenses for the family are below that, claiming them will not reduce your tax.

Add up all eligible expenses for the period, not only physiotherapy, before deciding whether to claim. The CRA page linked below has the current dollar figure.

## Receipts to keep

- Keep every receipt. The CRA says it must show the name of the company or person you paid.
- Keep your insurer's statements showing what was reimbursed.
- Do not send receipts with your return. Keep them in case the CRA asks to see them.
