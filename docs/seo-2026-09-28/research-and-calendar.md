Ahrefs' Canada counts for phrases containing “Winnipeg” are modelled national-database estimates, not a Winnipeg-only audience count. The neck, wrist and hip service pages should own the direct treatment searches; the blogs answer narrower preparation questions and link to those services. No keyword-difficulty or traffic forecast was available for these local terms.

## Website priorities after the draft review

1. **Improve the neck and surgical service pages with verified clinic detail.** GSC shows the neck service at 86 impressions/0 clicks/average position 41.9 and pre/post-surgical service at 35/0/38.8. Add what the real assessment covers, how patients prepare, and clinician-approved next steps. These service pages should handle direct treatment searches; the new posts answer narrower booking questions.
2. **Strengthen internal paths after publication.** Link each new post from its service page and a relevant older article. Do not link a published page to an unpublished draft. The ten drafts already link to the matching service, online booking and the clinic phone number.
3. **Measure enquiries per article.** Track organic landing sessions, booking-link clicks, phone-link clicks and confirmed enquiries by article URL. A click is an expression of interest, not a completed booking. The earlier audit found booking clicks but no confirmed `generate_lead` key event. Compare articles after enough traffic accrues rather than promising a conversion rate from Ahrefs volume.
4. **Keep the ACL correction separate.** The ACL post is already published and is not part of the ten new blogs. Its body still needs clinician review because of an absolute surgery claim and unsupported athlete chronology; a replacement draft preserves the URL.
5. **Do not fabricate a team biography.** The Himani profile lacks approved background, as noted in the prior technical crawl. Add only clinic-supplied qualifications and care information.
