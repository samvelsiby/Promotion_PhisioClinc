# Published blog content review — 28 September 2026

This is an editorial screening of the 24 articles that were published at the start of this review and the two posts published afterward, checked against the [College of Physiotherapists of Manitoba advertising direction](https://manitobaphysio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Advertising-and-Marketing.pdf), [Manitoba Health coverage information](https://www.gov.mb.ca/health/mhsip/healthcoverage.html), [MPI injury coverage](https://www.mpi.mb.ca/covering-your-medical-and-personal-expenses/), [WCB worker claim process](https://www.wcb.mb.ca/workers/report-your-injury/worker-claim-process/), and the clinical sources cited in the new posts. It is not a substitute for a registered physiotherapist's clinical review of each article.

## Priority changes in already-published articles

| Published article | Issue to correct before promoting or republishing |
| --- | --- |
| [ACL injuries and return to play](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/understanding-acl-injuries-recovery-timelines-and-the-path-to-ret1urn-to-play) | Absolute claims that complete ACL rupture requires surgery, plus named 2026 World Cup player examples that require independent verification. A separate correction draft already exists in Sanity; the live version remains unchanged. |
| [5 signs to see a physiotherapist](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/1-5-signs-it-s-time-to-see-a-physiotherapist) | Claims such as “identify the root cause,” “long-term solution,” “ensure tissues heal correctly,” and preventing future complications overpromise what an assessment or treatment can establish. The 3–5 day rule is presented as diagnostic. |
| [Recovering after a motor vehicle accident](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/recovering-after-a-motor-vehicle-accident) | “Hidden injuries,” manual therapy reducing inflammation, corrected alignment, full recovery, and early treatment preventing chronic issues are stated too confidently. Add medical warning signs and avoid implying automatic MPI coverage. |
| [Whiplash after a car accident](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/whiplash-after-a-car-accident-mpi-why-your-recovery-deserves-more-than-a-referral) | MPI allowing provider choice is supported; the statement that MPI may provide a “preferred” clinic list was not verified. Other text makes unsupported claims about clinic choice substantially changing outcomes and about “extensive experience.” |
| [Simple stretches for desk work](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/simple-stretches-to-ease-desk-job-tension) | Statements that the stretches prevent wrist strain or reliably reduce pain and improve posture need softening; exercise instructions should tell readers to stop if symptoms worsen. |
| [Exercise-based therapy and chronic pain](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/how-exercise-based-therapy-supports-chronic-pain) | “Root cause,” “most effective,” “exercise reverses these patterns,” and a safety guarantee are too broad for a chronic-pain population. Discuss exercise as one individualized option, with no fixed outcome promise. |
| [What is physiotherapy?](https://www.promotionphysiotherapy.ca/blogs/what-is-physiotherapy-and-when-should-you-see-a-physiotherapist) | Direct access to a private clinic is consistent with College guidance; fix “Wok place” typo and soften “prevent future injuries.” |

The other 17 articles did not show the same obvious promotional overclaims in this screening. The seven locally-authored articles in `lib/clinicArticles.ts` take precedence over their matching Sanity versions on the website and already contain cautious coverage language. Their exact published rendering should still be checked by the reviewing physiotherapist, especially clinical exercise guidance and any statements about insurer arrangements.

## Additional website item

The homepage displays patient testimonials in `components/Testimonials/Testimonials.tsx`. The College direction prohibits *soliciting* testimonials or web reviews and requires explicit informed consent before using patient images. The screening did not establish how these existing testimonials were obtained or whether display consent was recorded; the reviewing physiotherapist should check that history. Do not use GBP's “Get more reviews” action to solicit reviews.
